Bonds that repay themselves from a protocol's fees.

A protocol sells a short-term bond against its own future fee revenue. An agreed share of every fee is split off in the same transaction and paid to bondholders until face plus coupon is covered — then the split stops on its own.

Revenue-share token

A perpetual rent on the revenue. No capital arrives up front.

None of them trades a defined share of future revenue for a defined sum today, with a date when the obligation ends.

The fee is split where it arises.

  1. 01A swap on the issuer's protocol pays a fee.
  2. 02The issuer's fee instruction calls the split by CPI.
  3. 03The agreed share goes to the bond's escrow — in the same transaction. On the standing issue it is 12 %.
  4. 04Holders claim their share whenever they like.

No extra transactions, no external executors, no money passing through the issuer's hands.

Repayment ends by itself once face plus coupon is covered. The issuer can also buy the flow back early in one payment.

Sell before maturity. Keep what you earned.

A holder lists part of a position at a fixed price. Another wallet buys it for USDC in one instruction. What accrued before the sale stays with the seller; the buyer earns from the purchase on.

The transfer hook settles both sides before every transfer — including one sent from any wallet app, past this interface.

What it does not do.

  • No catalogue of issues, no risk metrics yet. Buying a bond is possible; judging whose it is, is not.
  • No admission threshold. In this version anyone can create an issue.
  • One issuer, and it is our own demo issuer. No live third-party protocol is integrated.
  • Devnet only. Settlement there is a test USDC of our own: you can watch the standing issue and its offers, but not get this USDC to trade.
  • No collateral of any kind. The only backing is the fee stream.
  • No KYC, no legal qualification, no transfer restrictions. The instrument looks like debt; its legal assessment is not part of this work.
  • No proof that revenue is not diverted. We see only what passes through the split.
  • The issuer changes its own code. The split is called from the issuer's fee instruction; there is no zero-integration mode.
